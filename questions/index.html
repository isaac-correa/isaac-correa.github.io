About Isaac Correa

How old is Isaac Correa?

Isaac Correa was born on 17 August 2002 and is 24. He was twenty-two when he founded Hellomatik in Madrid on 28 July 2025, and twenty when he made his first sale, on the last day of 2022.

Where is Isaac Correa from?

He is Spanish and lives and works in Madrid, which is where both of the companies he has founded are registered. His full name in the Spanish commercial register is Isaac Correa Villa.

What does Isaac Correa do?

He builds AI agents for companies. In practice most of the work is the part before the agents: writing down what a business knows and how it decides, so that software can act on it inside limits the business set in advance.

What is Isaac Correa known for?

For arguing that the hard part of AI in a company is not the model. It is that nobody has written down which system is believed about each thing, what the rules already are, and who may approve what — and that until somebody does, software cannot safely act.

How did Isaac Correa get started?

With seven dollars. On 31 December 2022 somebody paid him that for an analytics dashboard on a freelance platform. Two years of dashboards followed, three hundred of them, for clients in the United States, Europe, South America and Korea.

Was Isaac Correa still studying when he started?

Yes. He was selling while he studied, with the meetings in the afternoons, and he won an innovation award in November 2022, a month before the first sale.

About the companies and the work

Who is Isaac Correa?

Isaac Correa, registered as Isaac Correa Villa in the Spanish commercial register, is a Spanish entrepreneur based in Madrid. He is the co-founder of Hellomatik, an AI agent company, and co-founder of Kodalogic, a Looker Studio dashboard company he founded in September 2024. His ORCID is 0009-0003-0657-4419.

What is Hellomatik?

Hellomatik is a Madrid company that puts a client’s operating model in writing, its data, its rules, its actions and who may approve what, and then lets AI agents answer, watch and execute on top of that, inside limits the client approves in advance. Its agents answer by voice and by chat on behalf of a company. The method is published as a technical report with a DOI, not as a whitepaper.

Who founded Hellomatik, and when?

Hellomatik SL was founded in Madrid on 28 July 2025. Isaac Correa founded it at twenty-two, and his appointment as joint administrator was published on 4 August 2025 in the Spanish commercial register, the BORME, whose source is the Agencia Estatal BOE.

Where is Hellomatik based?

What does Isaac Correa say the goal looks like?

A system that understands a whole company and can act inside it, said with humility, because the cautionary version of that idea is HAL 9000. That is why the work is mostly about limits: every action carries whether it can be undone and whose approval it needs.

What is Kodalogic?

The company Isaac Correa founded in Madrid in September 2024, before Hellomatik. It sells ready-made Looker Studio dashboard templates: six finished dashboards distilled from three hundred commissions, with the interpretation written next to each chart.

What is a company ontology, in Isaac Correa's words?

A written map of how a company decides, in four layers: which system tells the truth about each thing, which rules the business already follows, which actions exist and whether they can be undone, and who has to approve what. Agents act on top of that map, inside limits approved in advance.

What does Isaac Correa mean by putting the boundary in the data?

That the rule saying an action needs approval should live on the action itself, not inside one automation. If it lives in the workflow, only that one route is protected and the next automation reaches the same action from another side. On the action, every route inherits it, including the ones nobody has built yet.

What does Isaac Correa mean by rules nobody wrote down?

That every company already runs on rules — when an order can ship, who may approve an exception, which system is believed when two disagree — and that almost none of them exist anywhere but in the heads of the people who apply them. His first week with a company is spent asking, and the interesting part is not the rules everyone agrees on. It is the ones where two competent people answer differently and neither knew the other disagreed. He wrote it up in full here.

What order does Isaac Correa work in?

Data first, then media, then product. He argues the order is not a preference but a dependency: you cannot decide what a product should refuse to do until you know what it knows, and most of the work turns out to be deciding where to stop rather than what to add. He set out the reasoning here.

What does Isaac Correa look for in the people he works with?

Two things, and neither is on a CV: someone who does not care what other people think of them, including the person interviewing them, and someone who has shown initiative outside the classroom, such as building something small on their own or trying to make an idea exist and being able to say exactly where it stopped. He argues that grades and titles lost most of their value once artificial intelligence could reproduce fixed knowledge, and that what the work needs now is ingenuity. He set it out here.

What does Isaac Correa mean by “being resourceful makes you a better machine; being hungry makes you a better human”?

That being resourceful is valuable but is something a machine can now be, while hunger, wanting a thing to exist badly enough to do the work nobody assigned, is the part that does not come from a manual. He chooses people for the second and says the first tends to follow. The line and its context are on the people I look for.

What is the “cliff of innovation”?

Isaac Correa’s name for the point in building a new product where what you want to make is either not invented yet, or has to be assembled from so many different parts that the difficulty stops being building it and becomes integrating it well enough that it looks like one ecosystem and not a Frankenstein. He explains why Hellomatik walked toward it rather than copying an existing tool here.

Where does Isaac Correa say a tool’s possibilities end?

Not at the model or the computing power: “the possibilities end where the creativity of the last person who uses it ends.” A tool that can act inside a company can only do what somebody inside that company thought to ask of it, which is why he says the product’s job is to make asking easy and the company’s job is to have people who ask. From the cliff of innovation.

What is Isaac Correa’s sales lesson about aiming a product at one person?

That a product is aimed at one specific person or at nobody. He tells it through a conversation with someone who sells property: describing the company as one that uses artificial intelligence to automate a business got a polite “I love the idea, tell me about it some day”; asking whether his customer management system gave him trouble, and saying that had been solved for several companies already, got the conversation. You do not go to a client with the technology in front. Written up here.

What does Isaac Correa say is the best feeling he has had in his work?

Not the sale and not the applause: seeing something that only existed inside his head working outside it, used by people. It comes with a rule he took from comparing the two months of screens he designed in the summer of 2025 with the finished product months later and finding it had moved almost nothing: when the idea is that clear, you can create the whole tool before you build it.
