How I work on a product

My role is the one I gave myself: I think, and I direct the product. This is the order I trust, and where it came from.

Data, then media, then product

In January 2025 I sat down to explain what I believed about building, and it came out as three words in an order: data, then media, then product. Not because data is fashionable, but because the other two are guesses without it.

Data is what the business already knows and cannot say out loud. Media is how you find out whether anyone outside the building cares. Product is what you are allowed to build once those two have answered. Do it in another order and you will build something beautiful for nobody, which is the most expensive mistake available.

Two months, screen by screen

The part nobody photographs: two months designing screen by screen. Not wireframes in a workshop, actual screens, one at a time, each one argued over until it earned its place.

A screen is a decision made visible. If you cannot say what decision a screen helps someone make, the screen is decoration, and decoration in software is not free: someone maintains it forever.

From phone calls to a map of a company

Hellomatik started as something small: a system to answer the phone in a restaurant. A voice, a brain and a pair of hands. The voice takes the call, the brain decides what the call means, and the hands do the thing.

The hands were the problem. To do the thing, a system has to know what things exist, which rules the business follows and who is allowed to approve what. That question turned out to be much bigger than a restaurant, and answering it properly is what became the method we published with a DOI.

HAL 9000, and why it is a warning

People ask what the goal looks like, and the honest answer is that the picture in my head is HAL 9000: a system that understands the whole company and can act inside it.

I say that with some humility, because HAL is a cautionary tale, and the reason it is one is the same reason our work is mostly about limits. A system that understands everything and answers to nobody is the failure mode, not the goal. That is why every action carries whether it can be undone and whose approval it needs, and why the permission table is the part I would show a sceptic first.

What we run today

The report states the scope rather than implying it: four systems in production, among them a logistics one with 24 rules over live warehouse and ERP data, with over 30 million operations and 460,000 locations, and a B2B sales one that recalculates 29,000 customers nightly over 14 million sales lines. A method with its scope written down is a method.
