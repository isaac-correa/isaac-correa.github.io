Where others have cited this work

Anyone can write about themselves. This page is the other kind of evidence: places where someone else, with no reason to do me a favour, used what I published as a source. There are three, and they are not all the same thing, so I separate them.

Cited by name: Mordor Intelligence

The market research firm Mordor Intelligence cites me by name in its report on AI in patient care and management. The entry reads: Isaac Correa, “Digital Front Door in Healthcare: Strategy Guide 2025”, Hellomatik. It sits in the reference list of the report, next to the other sources they used.

This is the one that matters to me, because it carries my name rather than a company’s. A market report is written to be sold to people making decisions with money; it does not cite you as a favour.

Used as a source: DentistryIQ

On 24 January 2026 the trade publication DentistryIQ published an article on missed dental appointments and linked to our figure on how much of a cancelled slot automated waitlists recover.

To be precise, because the difference is real: that article links to Hellomatik, not to me by name. What makes it worth recording is the company it keeps. The other external sources in the same piece are PubMed, PMC and Frontiers — peer-reviewed literature. We are in that list because the academy measures clinical outcomes and surveys measure preferences, and what actually happens when you automate a front desk was not being measured by either.

Used as a source: Dental Economics

Dental Economics did the same on 6 March 2026, in an article on patient self-scheduling: it cites our figure that 90% of dental offices are not using automated waitlists. Its other external source is PubMed.

Both trade publications ended up citing the same piece of ours, the one on automated waitlist management. That is the part worth understanding: they did not link to a product page. They linked to a number, because they needed one and it was not published anywhere else.

Why this happens

Almost everything written about AI in companies is a demonstration or a promise. The result is rarely shown, because the person selling has no interest in the denominator: how many times it failed, over how many attempts. So we run these tools inside a company with real invoices, stock and purchase orders, and publish what the numbers say, including where they fail.

That is also why the method is a technical report with a DOI rather than a whitepaper. A whitepaper is marketing that hopes to be believed. A report with a DOI is a fixed, dated text in a repository that is not ours, which anyone can quote and take apart.
