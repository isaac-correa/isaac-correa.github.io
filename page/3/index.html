Copy until you find creativity where there is none

Most advice about innovation says the same thing: be creative. I think that is the wrong instruction, and I run two companies on the opposite one. This is the short version, with the two rules I actually use.

Creativity is not something you look for

Nobody innovates by sitting down to be creative. Creativity is not an input you can decide to have. What you can decide to have is the ability to make better connections between things, and that ability comes from one place only: having lived things that do not resemble each other.

That is why I trust people with two careers that do not touch more than I trust anyone who has spent a whole life inside one sector. The specialist knows his field better than I ever will. But the person who has sold, built and studied unrelated things has more material to connect, and connections are where the new comes from.

Rule one: copy, and copy well

My advice to anyone starting out has nothing romantic in it. Copy others, as much and as well as you can, until you can find creativity where there is none. Copying well is not cheating. It is how you learn what already works, and it is the only honest way to notice the gap that nobody has filled yet.

Kodalogic, the dashboard company I founded in September 2024, came out of exactly that. We looked at three hundred jobs done for real clients, found the pattern that repeated in all of them, and kept the best of each. The product was not designed. It was distilled. The businesses we had looked at sold whole catalogues of them. We sold a handful, and they sold.

Rule two: do not look for gold, make it

The second rule follows from the first. Once you have copied well enough to see the gap, you stop looking for an opportunity somebody else left lying around. You make one. Hellomatik, the company I founded in Madrid on 28 July 2025, started as a system to answer restaurant phone calls and became a method for writing down how a whole company decides, published as a technical report with a DOI. Nobody left that lying around. It was made, one connection at a time, between dashboards, phone calls and the question of what a company actually is.

The people this works with

There is a kind of person this works with, and I have a one-line definition: people who see greys where the rest of us see blacks. People who find the opportunity where others see the setback. They do not solve problems and they do not look for solutions. They create them.

The machine of the future

I wrote this in November 2025, while building Hellomatik in Madrid. It is where I set down what I think the next tool is, and what it will always need from us. The machine of the future is the tool that can take actions in the real world when you write what you need, in plain language.

Why it arrives

It is reasonable to assume that, at some point in the evolution of technology, running a company will stop being a manual, fragmented activity spread across many separate tools. The historical pattern has always been the same: when a task is repetitive, can be expressed as rules and is necessary for an organisation to keep going, sooner or later it gets automated.

We can imagine, then, that creating a company will come down to switching on a central system: a platform that takes over the functions several human departments perform today. That platform will not just store data or execute orders, as today’s programs do. It will learn patterns, infer intentions and make operational decisions within set limits.

If a company needs to publish information, adjust its public presence, answer customers or coordinate suppliers, the system will not wait for detailed instructions. It will act. It will recognise what is worth doing given the circumstances and carry it out with the constancy of a machine and the flexibility of an experienced professional.

In practical terms, it will be like hiring not a tool but an administrative mind. An entity that coordinates, maintains and corrects the internal workings of a business. A company managing another company from the inside, without a body, without a timetable, without wear. Its only job will be to operate.

What that changes

If the whole management of a business can be concentrated in one platform, reachable from anywhere and able to carry out tasks on its own initiative, the barrier to starting a company drops sharply. In other times, starting an economic activity demanded capital, contacts, time and specialised knowledge. If much of that complexity is delegated to intelligent operating systems, anyone with a basic idea could try to become an independent worker.

That has a direct consequence: the number of independents rises. Not because of a cultural change, but a structural one. When costs fall, participation rises. We have seen it before in economic history, every time technology simplified production or distribution.

The same simplification has a side effect. If everyone has the same tools and the same automated technical capabilities, the differentiator is no longer manual skill, nor even specialised knowledge. It is the ability to shape something that others consider valuable. In other words: judgment, taste, and the ability to generate interest.

Companies will then look less like human organisations and more like systems of services interacting with each other. Many will exist only as digital structures that produce content, negotiate with other automated entities and keep themselves running without constant human presence. In that scenario, the main task of the individual will not be to do the work, but to decide what is worth doing. That is not a loss of humanity. It is a reconfiguration: human activity moves from execution to intention.

What stays human

The value of a company is the people who work in it. That will always be true, however far the technology goes. What is also true is that a company’s clients value it not for its headcount but for its ability to meet their needs. A company that can understand and fulfil what its clients need has value regardless of how many people run it. So the machine of the future is the one that lets a legal person depend a little less on physical persons for its existence. And all of it works only with a human component in its decisions.

From organisation to being

I would call a being any entity, or person, able to make decisions with a purpose behind them, knowing why. Today’s AI agents and chat products do not have that component. They execute without knowing why. They are programs, like all programs, that now meet needs in a simpler and more efficient way. Nobody has given them enough to decide what is best at each step. My bet is that a company can gradually evolve from an organisation into a being in that sense: an entity that decides with a purpose, and knows the purpose.

What the machine needs

First, memory to feed on context: the tools it is connected to, what they do, its personality, the situation, the knowledge of the company. That artificial brain must not be limited to what we give it at the start. It has to create records and memory of its own experience, so that it does its job better each time.

The most important thing inside that brain is a goal and a few basic principles to base its decisions on. The goal matters as much as the principles, because faced with a decision it will choose one option or another according to the values it was given. Most people, when they build an AI system, see it as a program that is a bit easier to operate. That is the wrong layer. Its real power is in giving it a purpose and rules for making decisions.

This matters most when it deals with humans, because human interaction is unpredictable. Think of sitting an exam in a subject you have never studied, with a three-hundred-page book in front of you. You can do it, but the result is imperfect. The next day you already sense which pages to look at. After enough mornings of the same exam you answer without opening the book. And faced with a completely new exam, you chain ideas from the previous ones into answers you had never given. That is what memory will do for this machine: turn knowledge into instinct.

Making a decision is the most human thing there is. To make one, a person weighs feelings, knowledge, needs, principles and experience. A company, to me, is like a child: if you look after it, it grows, and it needs you every day. Anyone who owns a small business knows that feeling: the wish to step away for a few days and come back to find the clients answered and the work done. What I am building is what lets that child grow on its own. Put without the metaphor: the difference between having a business and being tied to it.

From a seven-dollar dashboard to Hellomatik

My first sale was seven dollars, on the last day of 2022. Everything I run today starts there: Hellomatik, the company I lead in Madrid, Kodalogic, the company I founded before it, and a method with a DOI.

November 2022: the first applause

A team I was part of won a special recognition for the most innovative solution at an entrepreneurship competition. I was twenty, and it was the first time a room applauded something I had built. I barely remember what we presented.

The record does. The ceremony was streamed on 30 November 2022 and the video is still up: it runs for three hours and thirty-nine minutes, and I come on at 3:28:19. I give the timestamp because the point is not the prize. The point is that you can go and check it. That habit — say only things another person can verify on their own — is the one thing I have kept from then until now.

31 December 2022: seven dollars

On the last day of that year a client in the United States paid me seven dollars for an analytics dashboard on a freelance platform. I did the job as if it were worth a thousand, got paid, and closed the year looking at that number. It was the first of many.

2023 and 2024: three hundred dashboards from a desk

For the next two years I sold while I studied. I managed clients from half the world from my desk, with the meetings in the afternoon: the United States above all, half of Europe, South America, South Korea. Small businesses and companies of considerable size. Some days there were five meetings, and the deliveries went out in hours.

The prices went up as the work got better, and the numbers at the end are the ones I keep: three hundred dashboards delivered, and 103 verified client reviews.

The question that founded a company

By the three hundredth dashboard I could see the pattern. Three hundred clients had asked for the same thing: the same questions to their data, the same report, the same blank canvas to start from. The question I asked was not about the product. It was about access: why can only the people who talk to me have this?

I founded Kodalogic in Madrid in September 2024. Out of three hundred jobs for real clients, the catalogue is six finished dashboards, distilled from what repeated in all of them, with the interpretation written next to each chart. Everything Kodalogic has sold came from appearing in search results, and that took articles and patience: I wrote 58 of them, all signed. The first sale came from the United States, and that client still pays today.

In January 2025 the two of us were invited back to a university to explain the company to a hundred students. That is where I first put into words the order I still work by: first you are a data company, then a media company, and only at the end a product company.

From a phone call to a company’s ontology

The first idea for the next company was small: a system that answered restaurant phone calls. Then the idea grew. A dashboard gives a company its data. Why not talk to that data? Why not give a company a voice, and a brain? And the question that made it mine: why not give it hands too?

Before a single line of code, I spent two months alone with the idea. The first month deciding what it had to be and how it would be explained. The second turning that into a design, screen by screen, of a product that did not exist yet. Months later, with the tool running and in use, I compared the design with the real thing. Almost nothing had moved. When an idea is that clear, you can build the whole tool before you build it.

Hellomatik was founded on 28 July 2025 in Madrid. I was twenty-two. The name is a contraction of “hola” and “automatizaciones”. My role is the one I gave myself: I think, and I run the product. The reference I keep in mind is HAL 9000, an entity that understands a whole organisation and helps the people inside it, and I keep it as a reminder of how far there is still to go, not of how far we have come.

The first client was a large clinic. We gave the agents the whole content of the company, then a goal and an intention, so they would not just answer but resolve. Those agents are still running, and the clinic is still a client.

The idea kept evolving, from answering a call to writing down how a company decides: its data, its rules, its actions and who may approve what. In September 2026 we published that method as a technical report with a DOI, How we build a company’s ontology.

What selling it taught me

You do not lead with the technology. Nobody buys “we built an AI company that automates with your data”. What works is naming the client’s own problem: if your CRM is a mess, we fixed that for several companies, and we can look at yours. A product is aimed at one specific person, or it is aimed at no one.

How I think about innovation

Nobody innovates by sitting down to be creative. What you can decide to have is the ability to make better connections between things, and that comes from having lived things that do not resemble each other. So my advice to anyone starting out has nothing romantic in it: copy others, as much and as well as you can, until you can find creativity where there is none. Then you stop looking for an opportunity somebody else left lying around, and you make one.

There is a kind of person this works with, and I have a one-line definition: people who see greys where the rest of us see blacks. People who find the opportunity where others see the setback. They do not solve problems and they do not look for solutions. They create them.
